Mova Fitness App — MVP Feature & Screen Architecture

I've reviewed your Mova mockup. It already has a strong foundation: onboarding, personalized training plans, live workout logging, PR tracking, progress analytics, an exercise library, social features, and a profile/settings area.

My main recommendation is to build the MVP around one core user journey:

Set a goal → Get a training plan → Complete a workout → Track progress → Come back and improve.

Everything that supports this loop belongs in the MVP. Features that primarily create social engagement, competition, or visual polish can come later.

5. Recommended MVP navigation

I would actually make one small change to the navigation concept in your mockup.

Your mockup has Today, Train, Social and Me as its main navigation areas, with Progress shown as a separate screen. I'd promote Progress to a permanent tab and move Social to a later release.

Why this structure?

  • Today answers: What should I do?

  • Train answers: How do I train?

  • Progress answers: Am I improving?

  • Me answers: Who am I and how do I configure my app?

6. Features I'd avoid building too early

Some features look attractive in a mockup but can create considerable development work.

Feature

Why I'd postpone it

Social feed

Requires social graph, moderation and privacy

Gym rankings

Requires reliable gym/user data

Strength Score

Requires a defensible scoring algorithm

Health synchronization

Platform-specific integrations and permissions

Advanced analytics

Needs sufficient historical workout data

AI-generated plans

Adds complexity before validating basic plan usage

One particularly important point: don't make the strength score a launch dependency. Start with transparent metrics such as volume, completed workouts, exercise performance and PRs. You can introduce a score once you have a reliable calculation and enough user data.

8. Final recommendation: What should actually ship?

If I were managing the product, I'd aim for approximately 15–18 primary screen types for the first release, with reusable components and subviews rather than creating a separate screen for every action.

The launch version should let a user:

  1. Create an account.

  2. Set up their fitness profile.

  3. Select a goal and weekly schedule.

  4. Receive a training plan.

  5. Start a planned or custom workout.

  6. Search and add exercises.

  7. Log weights, sets and reps.

  8. Use a rest timer.

  9. Finish and save workouts.

  10. See their workout history and PRs.

  11. Track basic progress.

  12. Manage their profile and settings.

The most important product decision: Make the workout logger exceptionally good. It's the central feature that users will interact with repeatedly. Fast set logging, remembered weights, reliable workout recovery and effortless progression will provide more MVP value than a sophisticated social feed or leaderboard.

I'd validate those core behaviors first, then expand Mova into the social fitness platform suggested by your original mockup.
